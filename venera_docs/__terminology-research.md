# Terminology Research: "Cosmetology" — Russian vs English

**Date:** January 20, 2026
**Purpose:** Document the critical terminology difference that affects brand positioning

---

## The Problem

Dr. Venera uses "cosmetology" (косметология) to describe her practice, which is accurate in Russian but misleading in English. This creates a **credibility gap** where her medical expertise is undermined by terminology that implies non-medical beauty services.

---

## Findings

### In Russia (and Russian-speaking countries)

**"Косметолог" (Cosmetologist)** = A medical doctor

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Education Required | Full medical degree (MD), often with PhD |
| Training | Medical academy, 6+ years |
| Scope | Injectable treatments, medical-grade procedures |
| Regulation | Licensed as medical professionals |
| Hospital Departments | "Department of Clinical Dermatovenereology and Cosmetology" |

**Evidence from sources:**
- Russian medical clinics have departments titled "Department of Clinical Dermatovenereology and **Cosmetology**" headed by Doctors of Medical Sciences
- Russian practitioners are described as "Dermatologist, **cosmetologist**, PhD, M.D."
- "Medical education for a cosmetologist is a prerequisite for official work" in Russia
- Russian clinics state that "all procedures are performed by a specialist with medical education"

### In English-speaking countries (US, UK, Australia)

**"Cosmetologist"** = A beauty professional (non-medical)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Education Required | Certificate program (4-6 months) |
| Training | 250-1,000 hours of coursework |
| Scope | Hair, nails, makeup, basic skincare |
| Regulation | State cosmetology license (not medical) |
| Work Settings | Salons, spas (not medical facilities) |

**Evidence from sources:**
- "Cosmetologists are not medical professionals" but must undergo specific training for certification
- "There's a world of difference between a general cosmetologist and a medical cosmetologist"
- "The work of cosmetologists is surface-oriented and temporary"
- US estheticians need only "a high school diploma or GED certificate to attend cosmetology school"

---

## The Credibility Impact

| When Dr. Venera says... | English speakers understand... | Reality |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------|---------|
| "I am a cosmetologist" | "She does facials and maybe nails" | She's an MD who performs medical procedures |
| "Cosmetology clinic" | "A beauty salon" | A medical practice |
| "Cosmetology treatments" | "Spa services" | Injectable medical procedures |

This terminology mismatch **undermines her authority** and positions her incorrectly against actual physicians.

---

## Recommended Terminology

### For Dr. Venera's Title

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use Instead |
|----------|---------------|
| Cosmetologist | **Aesthetic Physician** |
| Beauty doctor | **Aesthetic Medicine Specialist** |
| Cosmetology doctor | **Injectables Specialist Physician** |
| | **Medical Aesthetician, MD** |

**Best option:** "Aesthetic Physician, MD" — clear, professional, medically authoritative.

### For the Practice

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use Instead |
|----------|---------------|
| Cosmetology | **Aesthetic medicine** |
| Cosmetology clinic | **Aesthetic medicine practice** |
| Beauty treatments | **Aesthetic treatments** |
| Cosmetology services | **Injectable treatments** |

### For Services

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use Instead |
|----------|---------------|
| Cosmetic procedures | **Non-surgical aesthetic procedures** |
| Beauty injections | **Medical-grade injectables** |
| Cosmetology treatments | **Physician-administered treatments** |

---

## Key Phrases to Emphasize

These phrases reinforce medical credibility:

- "Physician-led care"
- "MD-administered treatments"
- "Medical-grade products"
- "Non-surgical aesthetic medicine"
- "Certified doctor"
- "Injectable specialist"
- "Aesthetic physician with 10+ years experience"
